68. Lars GÅLMAN, son of Erik and Unknown, died about 1773. Another name for Lars was Lars ERSSON.

General Notes: He was a soldier and Gålman was his soldier name. He lived in Gårdö Soldattorp in Bo Parish. His patronymic name was Ersson. He was a in Gåhle in 1754. He moved there in 8 October 1745 at the age of 38. This means he was born between 1706 -1717.

We know he was dead by 1773 because his wife is called a widow in 1773.

Swedes did not have family "last" names. They were usually identified as being the son or daughter of someone. Lars' family was named Erik and the patronymic of Erik can be Ersson or Eriksson. These names may be used interchangeably. I have not been able to locate any records on Lars himself. All information I have found comes from records about his son or wife.

Lars married Brita ANDERSDOTTER .

124. Oluf Jørgensen Møller, son of Jørgen JØRGENSEN MØLLER and Unknown, was born in 1689 in Bierre Sogn, Vejle County, Denmark and died on 20 OCT 1774 in Bierre Sogn, Vejle County, Denmark, at age 85.

General Notes: Jørgen Møller had a son named Wolle in 1689 in Bierre Parish. Wolle is the same name as Oluf. I think that this is Oluf Jørgensen Møller's birth record. The reason I belive this is because there are no witnesses to the birth of Jørgensen Møller's children from any parish other than Bierre. Usually if the people are from another parish the majority of birth witnesses will be from that parish.

There is no proof that Oluf Jørgensen Møller is the father of Michel Olesen. Michel's birth record states that he is the Miller's son. Michel's father is named Oluf and his birth appears in the proper sequence for him to be the son of Oluf Jørgensen. There is also a correlation with the witnesses of all of Oluf Jørgensen's other children. Michel's birth is the only one which does not state the father's name. I intend to do further research into all the surrounding parish records.

Another problem with these records is that the some years are missing and some pages are torn and so badly damaged that they are impossible to read.
